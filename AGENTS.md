# tideline — the rules

The contract an agent extending this theme works under. `themes/CONTRACT.md`
binds first; everything below is either stricter than it or a declared,
argued deviation. A theme may be stricter and say so; it may never be looser.

## What this is

**Tideline is a pre-launch site, not a waitlist page.** That distinction is the
whole product decision and it is why the theme exists: twelve free waitlist
starters ship one screen with a form on it, and there is nothing to be better
at inside one screen. Six routes, a build log, an honest access statement and
five designed emails is a different object.

**The fiction**: Marram, an eleven-room house on the Dyfi estuary in Gwynedd,
opening May 2027. Nothing is bookable, no deposit is taken, and the site says
so in three places. Fiction domain: `pre-opening hospitality` — checked against
every shipped fiction and adjacent to none of them (covers is a *trading*
46-cover restaurant; the nearest thing here is a building site).

## The signature — the materials board

Five samples, photographed flat, each carrying the design token it **is**.
`--material-slate` is the colour of the slate in the photograph; the swatch on
`/components` and the plate on `/the-build` are the same decision rather than
two guesses that drifted.

It runs on `/`, `/the-build` and `/components` — three routes, past the ≥2
floor. It is not portable: a materials board only means something for a thing
that is being built, which is what makes it a signature rather than a stat
strip with pictures.

**Never** add a material to `MATERIALS` without adding the token, and never add
a token to the `--material-*` family that is not on the board. `materialFor()`
throws at build time if a room names a material the board does not carry —
that throw is the invariant, do not soften it to a fallback.

## The fixture spine

`src/fixtures/marram.ts` is the canonical source of every React figure.
`derive()` computes `7 of 11` once; the home page, `/rooms`, the build log and
email 04 all read it. **A figure typed into a component is a bug**, including
in the emails.

The HTML edition is a checked-in projection, not a live import:
`html/src/data/marram.json` repeats those facts because Nunjucks reads JSON at
build time, and there is no projection generator on disk. A fixture change is
therefore incomplete until that JSON is updated in the same change and both
editions have been compared.

## Invariants

- `src/theme.css` is the only file with a colour, a size, a duration or a
  radius. Components carry semantic utilities only.
- **In light mode, brass is a fill and never a graphic.** `#c9a227` on the
  cream ground is **1.88:1** — under the 3:1 graphic tier. Every brass *ink* —
  eyebrow rules, marks, links, figures — uses `--primary-ink` (5.35:1 light,
  10.42:1 dark). `text-primary` on a hairline is a defect, not a preference; the
  `INK` / `INK_BORDER` constants in `components/ui/type.ts` exist so nobody has
  to remember this.
- Both state ramps **lighten**. `--primary-foreground` is near-black ink in
  both modes, so lightening the brass is what raises its contrast
  (7.61 → 9.12 → 10.83). Darkening it in light mode measured **3.98:1** and was
  rejected. Recorded here because CONTRACT §Colour construction requires the
  direction be stated rather than assumed.
- Every `href` resolves to a real route. No `#` stubs.
- `data-brand-name` appears on the wordmark in the header and footer and
  nowhere else — it is the rebrand injection point.
- Do not add hand-rolled **icons**; `lucide-react` is the set. The brand mark
  is the exception and is not to be touched.

## Badges and icons — the policy, and it is short on purpose

`ui/badge.tsx` is the one badge shape and it has **two tones, not a palette**:
`quiet` (hairline, muted) and `marked` (brass ink on the accent wash, for the
one that is ahead — a wave-one room, a finished stage). The catalog's habit is
a tinted pill per status; this theme could not do that if it wanted to, because
`accent_mode` is `monochrome-plus-spot` and there is exactly one chromatic ink.

**Never colour alone.** Every badge keeps its word, and the ones naming a
lifecycle position keep a glyph too, so the marked/quiet difference is the
third signal rather than the only one.

**An icon has to be doing one of two jobs, or it does not ship:**

1. **Making a repeated row scannable.** The eleven rows of `room-table` each
   carry an aspect, an area, a bed count and a stage; four glyphs turn a wall
   of text into columns the eye jumps between. The stage ladder is the same
   argument — `Frame → Home → PaintRoller → Hammer → Check` reads as a sequence
   across eleven rows in a way five words do not.
2. **Naming a place or a time**, where the glyph is faster than the label: the
   station and the location in the footer, the arrival line on an email card.

**Everything else has none, deliberately.** No glyph in an eyebrow, none on a
section heading (the one exception is the access statement, whose whole subject
is the thing the glyph names), none in the hero, and **none on the materials
board** — the swatch is already the icon and a mark beside it would be the same
information twice.

The one moving glyph is the learn-more arrow on a "read on" link: +2px toward
the page it points at, hover only, per motion-pass's recipe.

## Typefaces

| Role | Family | Why |
|---|---|---|
| display | **Spectral** | Keeps the 81px display strokes sturdy. |
| text | **Manrope** | Keeps 13px copy legible on the near-black ground. |
| mono | **Martian Mono** | Makes dates, codes and figures read as measured evidence. |

All three self-hosted OFL woff2, latin subset, in `src/fonts/`.
`next/font/google` is forbidden: a build-time fetch breaks the offline zip.

## Colour — accent, and the closed list

`accent_mode: monochrome-plus-spot`, ADR-008 branch (b): the spot's own wash.
`--accent` sits at H 88.6, **1.1° from `--primary`'s 89.7** (inside ±15°) at
C 0.041, under half the primary's 0.138.

Its appearances are closed, and this is the complete list:

1. The ground beneath the brass kicker in an email card (`email-sequence`).
2. The ground of a wave-1 room row on `/rooms`.
3. The `--primary-soft` pairing behind a selected material row on
   `/components`.

It is never a decorative fill, never a section background, and adding a fourth
appearance means amending this list in the same commit.

## Motion — personality **tidal**

Slow, one-directional, nothing repeats. One entrance shape (`tideline-rise`:
12px lift, 380ms, house curve), micro-feedback at 140ms, and **no ambient
slot** — it is deliberately unspent, so this theme owes no WCAG 2.2.2 pause
control and there is nothing on any page that moves without the reader.

**The declared deviation.** motion-pass's vocabulary says entrances fire "on
first paint only". This theme fires each one **once, on first view**, via an
IntersectionObserver that unobserves in the same statement.

*What it buys*: this is a page whose argument lives two and three folds down in
photography. An entrance that already played before the reader arrived has
communicated nothing, which makes it decoration that costs a frame.

*Why it is not the banned thing*: the floor bans scroll **scrubbing** —
parallax, scroll-jacking, re-triggering. None happens here. There is no scroll
listener, each element animates exactly once, and scrolling back up replays
nothing.

*Why it cannot blank the page*: the resting style is the **finished** state.
The class that hides an element is added by the effect itself, so it exists
only in a document that is certainly going to animate. JS off, hydration
failed, or reduced motion set — all three render the complete page.

The hero has no entrance at all: it is above the fold, so a reveal there would
fire during first paint and read as a page that loads badly.

### The second deviation — scroll-driven media (owner, 2026-08-28)

motion-pass lists **parallax on marketing surfaces** under *what never
animates*, at floor tier. **The owner lifted it for this theme.** It is lifted
for this theme only; the rule stands for the rest of the catalog, and a future
theme wanting it needs its own directive rather than this precedent.

*What it buys*: the brief is a media-heavy pre-launch site. The land is the only
finished thing this house has to show, and a still photograph of it says less
than the same photograph with the dune travelling in front of the estuary — that
difference is the product.

**What was built, and the four constraints it is built under.** The hero, the
material rail and the room plates use CSS scroll-driven animation
(`animation-timeline: view()` / `scroll()`). `land-gallery.tsx` is the one
exception: an `IntersectionObserver` changes its active plate as ordinary
trackers cross the viewport, and its buttons scroll those same trackers. There
is still no scroll listener:

1. **The page's scroll speed is never touched.** Scroll-*jacking* changes how
   far the page travels per wheel tick; nothing here does. `End` reaches the
   footer, a keypress does what it always does, and every band moves exactly as
   far as the reader scrolls and stops when they stop.
2. **The finished state is the base style**, everywhere. Keyframes run *from*
   the offset position. `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` gates the
   whole block, so a browser without it gets the finished frame — not a broken
   one.
3. **Nothing starts on its own**, so no WCAG 2.2.2 pause control is owed. The
   ambient slot is still unspent.
4. **`transform` and `opacity` only.** Every animated element carries
   `will-change-transform`, and the compositor does the work.

The four devices, and where each lives:

| Device | Class | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Three-plane parallax | `tideline-layer-back/mid/fore` | `hero.tsx` |
| Pinned plate sequence | `sticky` + active plate state | `land-gallery.tsx` |
| Scroll-driven horizontal rail | `tideline-rail` | `material-rail.tsx` |
| Plate settling 1.12 → 1 | `tideline-settle` | `room-states.tsx` |

**The rail is not a carousel** and the distinction is load-bearing: no autoplay,
no timer, no arrows, no dots. Its track is `overflow-x-auto` in every case, so a
keyboard and a trackpad reach the far end whether or not the animation runs. The
motion sits on top of a control that works without it — it is never the only way
to reach the content.

**`svh`, not `vh`, on the land sequence's trackers.** On iOS `vh` is the
*largest* viewport, so with the address bar showing a `100vh` tracker is taller
than the screen and the last caption never finishes its timeline.

## Imagery

Twenty-three image assets, 21 JPEGs and 2 transparent PNGs, are AI-generated
with `.claude/skills/generate-image` and **regenerable**. The prompts are the
source in `src/assets/README.md`, and the shoot sheet is
`ops/design/art/shoot-tideline-2026-08-28.md`. One register (photographic), one
light direction (left, soft-raking), one colour story.

`tl-key.png` and `tl-layer-dune.png` are **chroma-key cut-outs**: generated on
flat green, keyed to transparency and despilled. Their contact shadows and
compositing stay in CSS, so both assets sit correctly on both grounds.

**The plates stay dark in both modes** — see the deviation below.

## The first sanctioned deviation

**The photographs do not invert with the page.** Light mode re-tokens
everything around them; the estuary plate, the room plate and the board stay
the dark exposures they were shot as.

*What it buys*: on a pre-launch site the photography is the product. A light
mode that re-graded the estuary at dusk would produce a washed second version
of the same picture that is worse in both modes, to satisfy a consistency
nobody asked for. The page inverts; the window does not.

*What it costs*: the light mode is not a bright page throughout — it is a cream
page with dark plates in it. That is a deliberate look, and it is the thing to
check first if the theme ever reads wrong in light.

## HTML edition boundary

The React and HTML editions share token values, fonts, imagery and canonical
facts. They share no markup or interaction code. `html/src/data/marram.json` is
a checked-in projection and must be updated with `src/fixtures/marram.ts`;
`bun run build-html tideline` does not generate that JSON.

The ledger is against what `html/src/` runs today. A dropped row names a real
reduction, not an implied browser behavior.

## Out of scope, declared

The marketing floor asks for a **pricing toggle** and a **testimonial**. Both
are declared **out**, not omitted:

- There is no price. The house does not open for a year, nothing is bookable,
  and a pricing table would be the site's first lie.
- There are no guests yet, so there are no testimonials. A pre-opening hotel
  quoting a delighted visitor is precisely the fabricated-social-proof tell the
  quality bar exists to reject.

What replaces them, and does the same job honestly: the **build log**
(`/the-build`) is the proof-of-progress a testimonial would stand in for, and
the **waves table** on `/list` is the pricing table's honest form — what you
get and when, with no number on it.

## Component library

Parts vendored from the repository's `ui/` library (tt-ui 1.3.0).
`src/components/ui/_tt/` and `html/src/js/_tt/` are **library-owned**: never edit
them. `bun run ui sync tideline` re-vendors them; `bun run ui eject <part> --theme
tideline` makes one this theme's own, moves it out of `_tt/` and flips its `state`
cell. Everything a buyer sees is decided in the sibling recipe file, which is this
theme's entirely. A dropped HTML-edition cell owes its sentence in
`## HTML edition boundary`.

`theme.json` lists all 14 vendored files on disk: 11 under the React `_tt/`
directory and 3 under the HTML `_tt/` directory.

| part | recipe | state | HTML edition | part version |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ui/accordion-disclosure` | `src/components/ui/accordion-disclosure.tsx` | vendored 1.3.0; used by the React FAQ and styleguide, not the React mobile nav | `_tt/accordion-disclosure.js` is vendored but not mounted; HTML FAQ and mobile nav remain bare `<details>` | 1.4.0 |
| `ui/field` | `src/components/ui/field.tsx` | vendored 1.3.0; used by the React join form and styleguide | `_tt/field.js` + `_tt/field-ids.js`, mounted through `html/src/js/join-form.js` | 1.2.2 |

## What never gets touched

- Never edit either `_tt/` directory. Use `bun run ui sync tideline`, or eject a
  part before changing library behavior.
- Never hand-edit `html/dist/`. Rebuild it from `html/src/`.
- Never remove the scoped selectors in `src/theme.css`, split the identical
  `--plate-ink*` values by mode, or use `--primary` as light-mode ink.
- Never replace the `materialFor()` throw with a fallback or type a derived
  figure into a component.
- Never flatten either transparent PNG onto a ground or discard the prompts in
  `src/assets/README.md`.
- Never add a route, section or primitive without updating `theme.json` and the
  literal `data-component` inventory on `/components` where applicable.
